River flow events

The Rangitata now runs down the far side of the gravel at Klondyke Bend, and now and then it swings across to the Klondyke Terrace side. Those swings are what put the road at risk, and they happen long before the bank itself shows any change — so they are worth watching on their own. Over the eleven years of satellite record the river has also been steadily moving away from the road, which is the first thing below.

The events

Two numbers, because they answer different questions. Typically is where the flow sat across most of the frontage on that pass — what you would see looking at the photograph. Worst spot is the single cross-section that came furthest over, which is what triggers the event. On the 0–1 scale, under 0.33 is hard right against Klondyke Terrace, 0.33–0.67 is the middle of the gravel, and over 0.67 is left, on the far side.

See it for yourself

Every event below is a satellite pass you can look at. The event frame is the pass on which the flow sat furthest onto the road side; the normal frame is an ordinary pass nearby, for comparison. Klondyke Terrace is drawn in red, water is tinted blue, and the braidplain the measurement is made across is shaded. Each cross-section runs from one edge of the braidplain to the other, with a small white dot at the halfway mark and a pink dot where the water actually sat — left of the white dot means the flow was on the far side, right of it means the road side. Click a frame to swap between the two, or use the button.
