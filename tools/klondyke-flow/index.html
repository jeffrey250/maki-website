River flow events

The Rangitata now runs down the far side of the gravel at Klondyke Bend, and now and then it swings across to the Klondyke Terrace side. Those swings are what put the road at risk, and they happen long before the bank itself shows any change — so they are worth watching on their own. Over the eleven years of satellite record the river has also been steadily moving away from the road, which is the first thing below.

Areas you want watching

Mark the parts of the riverbed that matter to you and give each one a name. Every area is checked straight away against all 448 clear satellite passes on record, so you can see whether the river has ever been there before deciding it is worth an alert. Press New area, then click around the area on the photograph. Scroll to zoom, drag to pan.
Areas are kept in this browser and are not sent anywhere. Export them to have MAKI set up alerting, or to keep a copy — the file holds plain NZTM2000 coordinates any surveyor or GIS can read.

How much ground is left

Distance from each of the three 2024 survey control points to the nearest water, on every clear satellite pass. The control points are fixed to about 4 mm and sit on the road, so this is simply how much bank stands between the river and Klondyke Terrace — and whether it is shrinking. How close is too close

Set the distance at which each control point matters to you. Each setting is checked against the whole record straight away, so you can see how often it would have gone off before deciding it is the right one — a level nobody has tested is just a number. The levels are drawn on the chart above.

When the river moved in

Occasions when the water came at least 25 m closer to a control point than it had been sitting around that time. “Usually” is the median for that point within a year either side, so an occasion is judged against the river as it was then, not against an eleven-year average of a river that has since moved.

See it for yourself

Every event below is a satellite pass you can look at. The event frame is the pass on which the flow sat furthest onto the road side; the normal frame is an ordinary pass nearby, for comparison. Klondyke Terrace is drawn in red, water is tinted blue, and the braidplain the measurement is made across is shaded. Each cross-section runs from one edge of the braidplain to the other, with a small white dot at the halfway mark and a pink dot where the water actually sat — left of the white dot means the flow was on the far side, right of it means the road side. Click a frame to swap between the two, or use the button.
