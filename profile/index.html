About MĀKI

We go where it's hard to measure

MĀKI is a New Zealand engineering technology company, founded in 2020 and based in the Waikato.

Our team of six designs, builds and operates autonomous systems (uncrewed survey boats, underwater ROVs, ground rovers, drones and IoT sensor networks) and turns the data they collect into answers for councils, utilities, contractors and researchers.

Most companies are strong in hardware or software. We do both in-house: mechanical, electronic and communications engineering alongside our own control software, data platforms and AI. That's why we can build the right tool for a job rather than make the job fit the tool.

Our mission is to make cutting-edge engineering technology accessible to the organisations that manage New Zealand's natural and built environments. That means collecting data safely and efficiently, including in places people shouldn't have to go.

How we work

The same team handles your job from the first call to the final data. There are no hand-offs between a hardware supplier, a survey crew and a data processor.

  1. 01

    Scope

    We review the site with you, agree the outputs you need, and choose the platform and method that suit the site, not just the one we happen to own.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Our operators deploy the USV, ROV, rover or drone with RTK positioning, working to our SHE Pre-Qual safety system. No divers, no confined-space entry.

  3. 03

    Process

    Sonar, imagery and sensor data are processed in-house into depth maps, sediment volumes, cross-sections and 3D models.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    Clear reports, GIS-ready data and, where it helps, an interactive 3D portal. We show how confident we are in every figure.

Case study · Western Bay of Plenty District Council

Eleven stormwater ponds, Ōmokoroa

The council needed to know how much water and sediment its Ōmokoroa stormwater ponds and wetlands hold, to plan and prioritise maintenance. Between June and August 2026 MĀKI surveyed eleven of them, matching the method to each site: multibeam sonar, sonar boat surveys, and RTK staff shots where a pond was too shallow or overgrown for a boat.

Each pond was delivered as a survey report plus a private online portal: a 3D model of ground, water and sediment, with a cross-section tool the council's engineers can use themselves. Every pond also shows how much of it lies close to a real measurement, so engineers know how far to trust each figure.

Case study · Waikato Regional Council

Sampling algal blooms without putting people on the water

During a cyanobacteria bloom the water can be toxic. Sampling from a boat or jetty puts staff at risk, and a handful of fixed sampling points says little about how the bloom is spread across a lake.

Since March 2024 we've worked with Waikato Regional Council's lake scientists to do this remotely. Our autonomous boat follows programmed routes across the whole lake, logging water quality as it goes. It lowers its sensors to set depths, staying clear of the lake bed, and its winch collects water samples of up to 3 litres for the lab. After the first trial in March 2024, the programme ran across several lakes over the 2024–25 summer.

0staff on the water during a bloom
6parameters, logged continuously
15 mwinch for readings and samples at depth
22.3 kmround trip in a single mission

Parameters: chlorophyll-a, phycocyanin (the cyanobacteria indicator), turbidity, dissolved oxygen, pH and temperature.

A tall antenna mast on the shore gives a direct radio link up to 2.5 km away. The boat now also carries 4G and Starlink, so it stays connected far beyond that, wherever there is mobile coverage or open sky. Every reading is tagged with its RTK GPS position and streamed back live, so the team sees results while the boat is still working.

Case study · Buller District Council

Inside a drinking-water reservoir, Westport

Inspecting a drinking-water reservoir normally means taking it out of service: isolating it, draining it, gas-testing the air and sending someone into a confined space.

For Buller District Council, MĀKI inspected the reservoir in Westport with the water left in it. Our ROV surveyed the inside of the reservoir underwater, while our rover carried out the inspection on top of it. Together they gave the council a full visual record of the asset.

Case study · Auckland Council · Glasgow Contractors

Under the jetty at Hobsonville Point

With Glasgow Contractors, on behalf of Auckland Council's Healthy Waters and Flood Resilience department, MĀKI completed a detailed bathymetric and topographic survey at Hobsonville Point on a tight timeframe.

Our pilot drove MĀKI's in-house USV, fitted with multibeam sonar, underneath the jetty, a confined space that crewed survey boats struggle to reach. An RTK drone captured the exposed coastal structures and surrounding land, and the two datasets were combined into one integrated 3D model.

Case study · Waikato Regional Council

26 Waikato lakes mapped

Waikato Regional Council needed consistent depth data across many of its lakes to plan sediment work, track lake volumes and assess habitat. Crewed survey boats are costly and often impractical on small or sensitive lakes.

Our autonomous boats follow programmed survey lines across each lake, logging depth with single-beam and multibeam sonar positioned by RTK GPS. The data is processed into depth grids and 3D models in the council's GIS formats, giving a baseline that can be re-surveyed to show change over time.

Innovation

Development roadmap

3-in-1 integrated platform

A USV that acts as an "aircraft carrier" for a drone and an ROV. Solar and hybrid power keep all three charged for long missions; the drone returns to its dock to recharge, and the ROV deploys from the boat's central bay. It's all operated wirelessly, with no one on the vessel.

Submerged pipe inspection & 3D modelling

A robotic crawler for wet or submerged pipes, with LiDAR, sonar and HD cameras. SLAM positioning works without GPS to build 3D point clouds and digital twins for defect finding and asset management.
