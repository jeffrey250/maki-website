About us

We go where it's hard to measure

MĀKI is a New Zealand engineering technology company, founded in 2020 and based in the Waikato.

Our team of six designs, builds and operates autonomous systems — uncrewed survey boats (USVs), underwater ROVs, ground rovers, drones and IoT sensor networks — and turns the data they collect into answers for councils, utilities, contractors and researchers.

Most companies are strong in hardware or software. We do both in-house: mechanical, electronic and communications engineering alongside our own control software, data platforms and AI. That's why we can build the right tool for a job rather than make the job fit the tool.

Our mission is to make cutting-edge engineering technology accessible to the organisations that manage New Zealand's natural and built environments — capturing data safely, efficiently and in places people shouldn't have to go.

Inspection · Monitoring · IoT

Eyes and sensors where people can't safely go

Underwater & confined-space inspection

Our ROV and rover inspect reservoirs, ponds, culverts, outlets and structures with live HD video — no draining, no divers, no confined-space entry.

Water-quality monitoring & sampling

Our sampling vessel profiles the water column with a precision winch: chlorophyll-a, phycocyanin, turbidity, DO, pH and temperature, plus 3 L samples from up to 15 m deep.

IoT & AI cameras

Solar-powered AI cameras detect pest animals such as wallabies at the edge, in remote places with little power or signal. Data flows to live dashboards and alerts.

Process

How we work

One team from the first call to the final data — no hand-offs between a hardware supplier, a survey crew and a data processor.

  1. 01

    Scope

    We review the site with you, agree the outputs you need, and choose the platform and method that fit — not the one we happen to own.

  2. 02

    Capture

    Our operators deploy the USV, ROV, rover or drone with RTK positioning, working to our SHE Pre-Qual safety system. No divers, no confined-space entry.

  3. 03

    Process

    Sonar, imagery and sensor data are processed in-house into depth maps, sediment volumes, cross-sections and 3D models.

  4. 04

    Deliver

    Clear reports, GIS-ready data and, where it helps, an interactive 3D portal — with the confidence in every figure shown, not hidden.

Case study · Western Bay of Plenty District Council

Eleven stormwater ponds, Ōmokoroa

The council needed to know how much water and sediment its Ōmokoroa stormwater ponds and wetlands hold, to plan and prioritise maintenance. Between June and August 2026 MĀKI surveyed eleven of them, matching the method to each site: multibeam sonar, sonar boat surveys, and RTK staff shots where a pond was too shallow or overgrown for a boat.

Each pond was delivered as a survey report plus a private online portal: a 3D model of ground, water and sediment, with a cross-section tool the council's engineers can use themselves. Every pond also shows how much of it lies close to a real measurement — so they know how far to trust each figure.

Case study · Buller District Council

Inside a drinking-water reservoir, Westport

Inspecting a drinking-water reservoir normally means taking it out of service: isolating it, draining it, gas-testing the air and sending someone into a confined space.

For Buller District Council, MĀKI inspected the reservoir in Westport with the water left in it. Our ROV surveyed the inside of the reservoir underwater, while our rover carried out the inspection on top of the reservoir — together giving the council a full visual record of the asset.

Case study · Auckland Council · Glasgow Contractors

Under the jetty at Hobsonville Point

With Glasgow Contractors, on behalf of Auckland Council's Healthy Waters and Flood Resilience department, MĀKI completed a detailed bathymetric and topographic survey at Hobsonville Point on a tight timeframe.

Our pilot drove MĀKI's in-house USV, fitted with multibeam sonar, underneath the jetty — a confined space that crewed survey boats struggle to reach. An RTK drone captured the exposed coastal structures and surrounding land, and the two datasets were combined into one integrated 3D model.

Development roadmap

What we're building next

3-in-1 integrated platform

A USV that acts as an "aircraft carrier" for a drone and an ROV. Solar and hybrid power keep all three charged for long missions; the drone returns to its dock to recharge, and the ROV deploys from the boat's central bay — operated wirelessly, with no one needed on the vessel.

Submerged pipe inspection & 3D modelling

A robotic crawler for wet or submerged pipes, with LiDAR, sonar and HD cameras. SLAM positioning works without GPS to build 3D point clouds and digital twins for defect finding and asset management.
