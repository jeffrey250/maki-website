Water Reservoir Inspection

Underwater ROV and surface rover inspection of reservoirs and submerged infrastructure — without dewatering, and without sending staff into confined spaces.

Inspect while the reservoir stays in service

Water reservoirs need periodic condition assessment to find structural deterioration, sediment build-up, biofilm growth and damage to inlet and outlet mechanisms. The conventional approach takes the reservoir offline and drains it — costly, slow, and disruptive to supply.

The alternative, sending staff into a partially drained reservoir, means entering a confined and potentially oxygen-depleted space, with all the hazard management that involves.

MĀKI deploys an underwater ROV and a surface rover instead. The reservoir stays full and in service, and nobody has to go inside.

What we deliver

  • High-definition video of the reservoir floor and walls
  • Still imagery of submerged structures, including inlet screens and outlet valves
  • The waterline, internal walls and access structures above it, captured by the surface rover
  • Live video during the inspection, so areas of concern can be examined more closely on the spot
  • A visual record ready for condition reporting, maintenance planning and renewal programmes

No dewatering

The reservoir remains in service throughout the inspection — no interruption to water supply and no pumping cost.

No confined space entry

The vehicles are operated from outside the reservoir, so no staff enter a confined or oxygen-depleted space.

Full coverage, one deployment

The ROV covers the floor, walls and submerged structures; the surface rover covers the waterline and above. Together they record the full internal surface.

HD video record

High-resolution video and imagery give your engineers a record to review, compare against future inspections and use in reporting.

Rapid mobilisation

Compact vehicles that deploy quickly — no draining schedule to plan around.

Built in-house

MĀKI designs its own hardware, electronics and software, so the vehicles and data workflow can be adapted to your asset.

Assets we inspect

  • Water supply reservoirs — walls, floor, inlet screens, outlet valves and access structures
  • Tanks — internal condition without taking the tank out of service
  • Pipes — submerged and confined sections that are hard or unsafe to reach
  • Dams and submerged structures — underwater faces, intakes and outlets
  • Stormwater infrastructure — inlets, outlets and structures in ponds and basins (see stormwater pond surveys)

How a MĀKI reservoir inspection works

1. Planning. We confirm access points, the structures you most need to see, and any requirements your organisation has for working in the asset.

2. Underwater inspection. The ROV is deployed into the reservoir and navigates the submerged zone, recording the floor, walls and submerged infrastructure. The live feed lets the team direct the ROV to anything that needs a closer look.

3. Waterline inspection. The surface rover records the internal walls and access structures at and above the waterline, completing the picture from waterline to floor.

4. Delivery. You receive the video and imagery record for condition reporting and maintenance planning.

Frequently asked questions

Can a water reservoir be inspected without draining it?

Yes. MĀKI inspects reservoirs while they remain in service. An underwater ROV captures high-definition video and imagery of the floor, walls and submerged structures through the water, so the reservoir does not need to be taken offline or dewatered.

Does anyone need to enter the reservoir?

No. The ROV and surface rover are operated from outside the reservoir, removing the need for staff to enter a confined, potentially oxygen-depleted space.

What does a reservoir inspection deliver?

A visual record of the reservoir's internal condition from waterline to floor: high-definition video and still imagery of walls, floor, inlet screens, outlet valves and access structures, ready for condition reporting, maintenance planning and renewal programmes.

What else can MĀKI inspect underwater?

As well as water reservoirs, MĀKI inspects tanks, pipes, dams, stormwater structures and other confined or submerged assets using ROVs, USVs, drones and imaging systems.

Where does MĀKI operate?

MĀKI is based in the Waikato and operates nationally across New Zealand.
